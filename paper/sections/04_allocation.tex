\section{Cost Allocation Strategies}
\label{sec:allocation}

Cross-cloud optimization is not only a pricing problem; it is also an accountability problem. Many billing rows are directly attributable to a team or workload, but platform subscriptions, shared networking, observability, and other common services often are not. The allocation layer therefore has two goals: preserve the economic total exactly, and project shared or unattributed spend into accountable analytical scope without hiding uncertainty.

The implemented system distinguishes direct allocation from shared-cost allocation. Direct allocation uses explicit ownership signals such as tags, subscription metadata, or team mappings. Shared-cost allocation applies when a cost is intentionally communal and should be fanned out across recipients using a stated basis. Unresolved unattributed spend remains visible rather than being silently forced into a misleading owner bucket.

\subsection{Shared-Cost Distribution}
Let $C_s$ denote shared cost and let $w_i$ denote the weight assigned to recipient $i$ such that $\sum_i w_i = 1$. The allocated shared cost for recipient $i$ is
\begin{equation}
A_i = C_s w_i.
\end{equation}
The core invariant is
\begin{equation}
\sum_i A_i = C_s,
\end{equation}
which prevents the allocation layer from creating or destroying cost.

In the current benchmark, Azure shared-cost fan-out preserves totals exactly, with zero delta between pre-allocation shared NEC and allocated shared NEC in every observed month. That result matters because it validates the accounting invariant before recommendation logic consumes the allocation outputs.

\subsection{System Integration}
Allocation outputs are treated as first-class decision features rather than as reporting-only artifacts. Team NEC summaries, shared-cost reporting, governance signals, and recommendation ownership all depend on the same allocation state. This design prevents a common operational failure in which dashboards, anomaly detection, and action queues each use a different notion of ``owner.'' The result is a more defensible bridge from billing visibility to actionability.
